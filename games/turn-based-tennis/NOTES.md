# Turn Based Tennis

## How to play

You return serve for one whole game of tennis. Each time a ball comes at you, the game pauses it and shows where it will land and some word clues; pick your Move, Stroke, and Shot within 10 seconds, then see how the Rule Book judged you and what happened.

It is a phone game. There is no home screen: the first serve comes as soon as the page opens (after the arcade password, if it asks). Tap the buttons; the pause button is at the top right.

## The screen: fixed zones

The play screen has three zones that never change size, so the court stays the same size and in the same place all game:

1. **Top bar:** a one-line score box ("Them 15 | You 30"), the seconds left, and Pause. The score goes 0, 15, 30, 40, then Win. At deuce both sides show 40, and the side with advantage shows Ad. The number bumps when that side wins a point.
2. **Court:** drawn as if you stand behind your own baseline, so it fills the phone's width. Everything you need to read is on the court.
   - **Players:** two small pixel sprites, with no name labels. You are seen from behind: black hair, brown skin, white shirt, burgundy shorts. They face you: teal cap and shirt, white shorts. Each sprite is a grid of 20 by 20 letters in `SPRITES` near the top of the drawing code, one letter per colored square, so you can change a square by changing its letter. Each has four pictures: ready, forehand, backhand, and a fist pump. You swing with the stroke you picked, they swing as the ball leaves them, and whoever wins the point raises a fist until the next ball. The court itself is unchanged.
   - **Their ball:** a dashed yellow line with a ring and ✕ where it bounces. After the bounce, a short stub shows its direction. The stub is the same length on every ball, so it never hints at Move in vs Stay. On a rally ball, a ring past your service line means the ball is short: move in. Serves always land in the service box. Move in is still a choice on them.
   - **Clue card:** when the ball pauses, a small card sits right beside it, on the side facing the middle of the court. A rally ball shows pace on top (Fast, Medium, Slow) and bounce below (High, Waist, Low). A serve shows the kind of serve. If the card would cover the path, it moves up, then down, then to the other side. It disappears once you answer.
   - **Your hit:** after you answer, their path fades, your circle runs to the ball, and hits it from beside it (forehand or backhand side). Your shot is a blue line, red with a ✕ if it misses. Your circle stays where you hit the ball while the card shows, then glides to your recovery spot as the next ball comes in (see "Smart recovery" below; on a new point you go to the return spot). The run is only for show: the Move answer is still judged from your fixed spot, as in the plan. If time runs out, you don't move and the ball goes past you.
   - **Point result:** when a point ends, a small label sits where the ball ended up: "You won it" (green) or "They won it" (red), and the score box bumps for whoever won. When you couldn't reach the ball, the label starts with the reason: "Too far", "Wrong way", "Two bounces", "Wrong stroke", or "Jammed" (for example "Too far · They won it"). The court itself shows why (the ball in the net, past you, landing out, and so on). There's no label while the rally goes on.
3. **Panel:** one fixed-size box. While you choose, it shows one question at a time with big buttons, three dots for your place, and your earlier picks at the top right. The Move buttons sit like a compass (you changed this): Move in at the top, Move back at the bottom, Step left and Step right on their sides, and Stay in the middle in a slightly lighter green. The four corners are empty, and the panel keeps the same height. The Stroke buttons sit on the side the ball comes to: Backhand and Run around backhand stacked on the left, and one tall Forehand button on the right. After the ball, it shows one line per question, led by a symbol in a small colored circle: ✓ for best, ~ for okay, ✗ for wrong. A wrong or okay answer gets a second line with the best answer and a short reason (for example "Best: backhand · ball on your left"), so you see it without tapping. The lines are just information: they aren't tappable.  If time ran out, unanswered questions say "No answer".

## From the plan

- **The turn loop (the part the plan cares about most):** the opponent serves or hits, the ball flies and pauses about 80% of the way to its bounce, the clues appear, the 10-second timer starts, and the three questions light up one at a time.
- **What the player is told:** the court marks the landing spot (ring and ✕) and draws the full path, including the part after the bounce. The clue card beside the paused ball shows the kind of serve, or a rally ball's pace and bounce, so everything you need is on the court. You changed this from the plan: there are no "where it's aimed", "side", or "depth" tags, because they made the game too easy. You read aim, side, and depth from the landing spot on the court (short balls land past the service line; deep balls land near your baseline).
- **Three choices and the 10-second timer (the plan had 8; you tried 10, went back to 8, then settled on 10):** Move, Stroke, Shot, one at a time. The answer is sent as soon as Shot is picked. If time runs out, you never hit the ball: it keeps going on its line, past you, and the point goes to them. Unanswered questions count as wrong on the card. You changed this from the plan, which had the ball go back with a 10-point higher miss chance.
- **Move and Stroke are judged where the ball reaches you (you changed this from the plan):** the plan used where the ball bounces. Now both use where the ball reaches you after the bounce, since that's where you hit it (serves angle toward your right, so this matters most on serves).
  - **Stroke:** if the ball reaches you within 1.5 ft of straight-on, it comes "straight at you" and forehand and backhand both count as best (`bodyZoneFeet`). Running around the backhand is okay on a close ball (6 ft or less) that is easy: on a rally ball, not fast and not deep; on a return, only a slow second serve (you changed this; spin serves used to count too).
  - **Move (three bands instead of the plan's one 4 ft line):** closer than 3 ft, Stay is best. From 3 to 6 ft, stepping that way is best and Stay is okay, because you can stretch. Farther than 6 ft, stepping that way is best and Stay is wrong (`moveStayFeet`, `moveReachFeet`). Short balls still mean Move in, and deep fast balls near you still mean Move back. Deep, high-bouncing balls near you also mean Move back, with Stay okay, so the bounce clue matters for Move (you added this). If a deep high ball comes 3+ ft to the side, stepping that way is best and Move back is okay. On a slow second serve that comes right at you, Move in is best (step in and take it early) and Stay is okay; if it comes 3+ ft to the side, stepping that way is best and Move in is okay.
- **Rule Book:** Move, Stroke, situation label, and Shot are judged as in the plan's tables, with one change you made: there is no "Approach the net" shot. The game can't show net play yet (you always recover to the baseline and there are no volleys), so it was really just a shorter down the line. Down the line is now the one best answer on a weak serve, a short ball, and a sitting ball. Crosscourt stays okay on the weak serve and the sitting ball, and drop shot stays okay on the short ball. Coming to the net could come back later as part of net play. Best = 2, okay = 1, wrong = 0, for a turn score of 0 to 6.
- **Does your shot go in? (you changed this from the plan):** random clicking was getting the ball back 71% of the time, so the plan's turn-score miss table was replaced. Every shot starts with a 3% miss chance, and each answer that isn't the best adds its own cost: a wrong move +25%, a wrong stroke +35%, a wrong shot +20%. An okay answer adds less (+8%, +10%, +7%). A tough rally ball (deep and fast, or wide and fast) or a fast flat serve adds another +10%, because a hard ball is harder to get back even with the right answers. With perfect answers, a tough ball or fast flat serve goes in about 87% of the time and every other ball about 97%. Random clicking gets about 45% in overall (about 33% on tough balls). All of these numbers are in `playerMiss` in `GAME_SPEC`; raise the costs to make it harder.
- **Automatic misses (you added this):** some mistakes mean you can't reach the ball at all, so you never hit it. The ball goes past you, and the point goes to them. There are five, each switched on in `autoMiss` in `GAME_SPEC`:
  - **Out of reach:** the ball reaches you farther to the side than you can run in time: more than 14 ft for a fast ball, 16 ft for medium, 18 ft for slow (`reachFeet` in `GAME_SPEC`). It's their winner even with the right answers. Your circle chases it and falls short. From the center this happens on about 1.4% of rally balls, after a crosscourt shot on about 4%, and after a down-the-line shot on about 6%, so smart recovery matters.
  - **Wrong way:** the ball reaches you 3 ft or more to one side and you step the other way.
  - **Two bounces:** a short rally ball and you pick a wrong move: stay, move back, or a sideways step away from the ball. Move in is best. If the ball comes 3 ft or more to one side, stepping toward it is okay too (you run in on an angle), so it isn't a miss. The ball hops a second time in front of you.
  - **Wrong stroke on a wide ball:** a rally ball lands 9 ft or more out to one side and you pick the other side's stroke (or run around a wide backhand).
  - **Jammed:** a deep, fast rally ball and you move in.

  Your circle makes the move you picked while the ball goes by. You never hit the ball, so the card doesn't grade your Stroke or Shot: those rows show a gray dot and "didn't reach it", and they don't count on the end-screen report card. (On a wide ball where the wrong stroke is the reason, the Stroke keeps its grade.) With these rules, random clicking gets about 34% of balls back in.
- **Smart recovery (you changed this from the plan):** the plan had you recover to the center of your baseline after every shot. Now you recover to the middle of the replies your opponent can hit, which depends on your shot. Crosscourt: 2 ft from center on the side you hit from. Deep middle: center. Down the line: 3 ft past center toward the other side (lob: 1 ft on your side; drop shot: 2 ft past center). The next ball's distance and side are measured from there, so down the line leaves you more court to cover. The numbers are in `recovery` in `GAME_SPEC`. The opponent aims at your open court (see below), so this matters.
- **Alternating serve sides (you added this; the plan served every point to the same side):** as in real tennis, the server switches sides every point. When an even number of points has been played (0–0, 15–15, deuce…), the serve goes to your right-hand box (the deuce side) and you stand 6 ft right of center. When it's odd (15–0, 30–15, advantage…), it goes to your left-hand box (the ad side) and you stand 6 ft left. Ad-side serves are a mirror image, so a T serve comes to your forehand and a wide serve pulls you to your backhand. There's no on-screen deuce/ad cue; your starting spot shows it.
- **The opponent aims at your open court (you added this; the plan placed rally balls at random):** your open court is the side of center farther from where you stand. Their tough balls go there 75% of the time, normal balls 60%, and easy balls 40% (`openCourtAim` in `GAME_SPEC`). Their winners always go into the open court. If you recovered to dead center (after a deep middle), there's no open side and they pick a side at random. In simulations, "too far" happens about twice as often after down the line as after crosscourt. A perfect player breaks serve about 95% of the time.
- **Opponent tables:** the opponent's miss and winner chances still use your turn score (0 to 6). The winner chances come from the plan. You changed the miss chances: the plan went up to 25% after a perfect turn, which let a perfect player break serve 97% of the time after only about 3 balls a point. They then went 3%, 4%, 5%, 7%, 9%, 11%, 13% (score 0 to 6), and a 4.0-level player broke serve only about 1 game in 4. You raised them 60% to 5%, 6%, 8%, 11%, 14%, 18%, 21% so a 4.0-level player (one who picks the best answer about 87.5% of the time) breaks serve about half the time. In simulations: 70% best answers wins about 9% of games, 80% about 26%, 85% about 42%, 90% about 60%, and a perfect player about 95%. Rallies last about 3 to 4 balls for a strong player.
- **How a miss looks:** the game blames one of your mistakes (bigger costs are more likely to be blamed), and that sets how the ball misses. A wrong move means you were late: net or wide. A wrong stroke means a mishit: net or long. A wrong shot, or bad luck on a perfect turn, misses the way that shot usually does (from the plan: a lob goes long, a drop shot hits the net). The miss is drawn at the right spot with a red ✕.
- **Where your shot goes:** the shot targets from the plan, based on which side you hit from. The opponent runs to cover your shot.
- **Left corner and Right corner (you changed this from the plan):** the plan's "Deep crosscourt" and "Down the line" answers were replaced by "Left corner" and "Right corner", left and right as you see the court. The game works out what the corner is from where the ball reaches you: hit from the right side of the court, the left corner is crosscourt and the right corner is down the line, and the other way round from the left. The Rule Book still thinks in crosscourt and down the line, and the corner gets that mark, so picking the wrong corner when crosscourt is best is the same as picking down the line. How the shot misses and where you recover also follow what the corner really was. If the ball reaches you within 1.5 ft of the center of the court (`middleZoneFeet`), neither corner is crosscourt: either corner gets the better of the crosscourt and down-the-line marks, and you recover to dead center. This happens on about 8% of rally balls. The "Best:" line names the button to press, for example "Best: left corner", because mixing button names and tennis words ("left down the line") was confusing. From the middle zone it says "either corner". Lob and drop shot still go to a side based on where you hit from.
- **Next-ball odds:** your last turn score sets the chance of an attackable, tough, or normal rally ball.
- **Game scoring:** 0, 15, 30, 40, deuce, advantage, shown in the score box at the top. The opponent serves the whole game.
- **Feedback after each turn:** what happened (a label on the court when a point ends) and each answer with a Best / Okay / Wrong tag. Wrong and okay answers show the best answer and a short reason right away. You changed this from the plan: the situation label (attacking, neutral, defending) and the turn score are not shown. The game still uses both behind the scenes to judge the Shot and to set the odds.
- **End screen (you simplified this from the plan):** who won, the points ("You 0 · Them 4"), a report card with one bar per question showing how many of your answers were the best one (for example "Stroke 4/9"), and Play Again. The plan's decision score and the list of every answer to work on were removed because the screen was too dense. Okay answers and time-outs count as not best on the bars.

Every number and table is in `GAME_SPEC` at the top of the code, so the odds and rules are easy to tune.

## Choices I made

- The return spot is on the deuce side (6 ft right of center), so every serve lands in the right-hand service box.
- "Distance" and forehand/backhand side are measured from the player's spot (the return spot for serves, the center spot for rally balls) to the landing spot.
- Rally ball details the plan left open: attackable balls are short or mid-court with a mostly high bounce. Tough balls are half deep-and-fast, half wide-and-fast, with a low or waist-high bounce. Normal balls are medium pace, deep or mid-court, mostly waist-high.
- The opponent serves from just left of center (on your view) behind their baseline.
- An opponent winner lands near a sideline, deep, and goes past you.
- The opponent hits their next ball from where your shot landed.
- After each turn you tap Next to go on, so you have time to read the feedback.
- The summary card shows only a short reason under each wrong or okay answer, so the feedback fits the same box as the questions and the court never resizes. The full reasons (`why` in the code) are no longer shown anywhere; they're kept in the code in case you want them back.
- Every Rule Book reason has a short version (`short` in the code) for the summary card, next to the full one (`why`). The "Best:" line also uses short shot names ("left corner", "middle") so it fits.
- There is a Pause button, because the build rules ask for one.

## Look (kept plain on purpose)

The plan leaves the look for later, so it is simple: a blue hard court on green seen from behind your baseline, a yellow ball, a dashed yellow line for their ball, a blue line for your shot, and red for misses. Marks use words and symbols (✓ Best, ~ Okay, ✗ Wrong), not just color.
